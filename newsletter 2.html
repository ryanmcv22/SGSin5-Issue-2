SGS Kicks Off Indigenous History Month With Hoop Dancing, Song, and Storytelling

SGS came together this week for a special Senior School Assembly marking the start of National Indigenous History Month, a month dedicated to recognizing the cultures, histories, and contributions of First Nations, Inuit, and Métis Peoples across Canada.

The morning opened with a welcome song and land acknowledgement from Mr. Alec Dan of the Musqueam Nation, setting a tone of respect and reflection for the whole school community.

The highlight of the assembly was a performance by hoop dancing duo Eli Rattlesnake and Teal Eaglespeaker. Eli is Anishinaabe and Teal is Blackfoot, and before taking the floor they walked students through the deeper cultural meaning that hoop dancing holds within their respective communities. The hoops represent the interconnectedness of all living things, and the performance that followed blended artistry, tradition, and storytelling in a way that left a lasting impression on everyone in attendance.

It was a powerful reminder of the living, vibrant cultures present right here in our community and a meaningful way to open a month of learning and recognition.
